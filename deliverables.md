## The final brief

Each team submits one Urban AI Decision-Support Brief on a real urban issue — inequity in service access, traffic
congestion, flood or heat exposure, housing, whatever your team chooses and can scope. Required sections:

1. **The urban issue** — what is happening, to whom, and why it needs a decision now.
2. **Site analysis** — the place, its conditions, and the evidence you gathered.
3. **Stakeholders** — who is affected, who decides, and who has been left out of past decisions.
4. **Decision criteria** — the criteria, their weights, and the justification for those weights.
5. **Scenario comparison** — at least two scenarios, compared on the same criteria.
6. **AI output evaluation** — what the AI gave you, what you checked, and what you found wrong.
7. **Responsible AI review** — bias, privacy, uncertainty, and limitations, named specifically.
8. **Human oversight plan** — who checks what, when, and against which source.
9. **Recommendation** — one recommendation a non-technical stakeholder could act on, with its conditions.
10. **AI use disclosure** — a summary drawn from your AI Use Log.

Target length is 8–12 pages including figures. Longer is not better.

<div class="warn">
  <strong>Two non-negotiables</strong>
  <p>Every factual claim must be traceable to a named source a reader could check. And you must show at least
  one place where human judgment overrode or corrected what the AI produced. A brief with no such moment has
  not demonstrated the course's core competency.</p>
</div>

## Completion

Non-credit completion requires attendance at 7 of 8 meetings, all six deliverables submitted, and a current AI
Use Log. Completing teams receive a certificate and a Spartan Experience Record entry. The top five briefs, as
scored on the rubric by the instructional team, receive gift cards.
